🦠 Why Viruses Aren't Alive

Viruses have genetic material and can evolve, but they cannot process energy independently, have no cellular structure, and cannot maintain homeostasis or reproduce without a host. They fail properties 3 (independently), 5, 6, and 7 — making them fascinating, but officially not alive.

How Science Works

The Scientific Method

Science uses a systematic approach to generate reliable knowledge. Tap each step to expand it.

1
Observation
Notice something interesting in the natural world. Observations can be qualitative (descriptive) or quantitative (measured). Good science begins with careful, objective observation — not assumptions.
2
Question
Form a testable question based on your observation. Not all questions are scientific — "Is this beautiful?" cannot be tested. Good questions lead to hypotheses that can be falsified.
3
Hypothesis
A tentative, testable explanation for the observation — an "educated guess" that makes a specific, falsifiable prediction. Written as an "if…then…" statement. Note: a hypothesis is NOT a guess; it must be based on prior knowledge.
4
Experiment / Test
Design a controlled experiment. Independent variable (what you change), dependent variable (what you measure), controlled variables (everything else kept constant). Experimental group receives the manipulation; control group does not.
5
Data Collection & Analysis
Record quantitative or qualitative data systematically. Use statistics and graphs to find patterns. Good data is repeatable, unbiased, and accurate. Peer review checks for errors.
6
Conclusion
Does the data support or refute the hypothesis? A hypothesis can never be "proven" — only supported or falsified. If not supported, revise and retest. Publish results so others can replicate them.
💡 Inductive vs. Deductive Reasoning

Inductive: Specific observations → general conclusion. "Every crow I've observed is black, therefore all crows are probably black." Used to form hypotheses.

Deductive: General principle → specific prediction. "If all birds have feathers, and a crow is a bird, then a crow has feathers." Used to design experiments.

📌 AP tip: Science is largely inductive (observations first). Experiments test deductive predictions derived from hypotheses.

⚠️ Hypothesis vs. Theory vs. Law

Hypothesis: Testable, falsifiable explanation for one observation.

Theory: Well-substantiated explanation supported by extensive evidence across many experiments. In science, "theory" ≠ "guess." Evolution is a theory.

Law: Describes a pattern in nature (e.g., Law of Gravity) without explaining why it occurs. Laws describe; theories explain.

⚠️ AP Exam Tip — Phylogenetics

Two species that share a more recent common ancestor (closer node) are more closely related — regardless of how far apart they look on the page.

Don't be fooled by branch length or visual distance. Only node positions determine relatedness.

🔬 Basic vs. Applied Science

Basic science: expands knowledge for its own sake (no immediate application expected). E.g., studying how DNA replication works.

Applied science: uses scientific knowledge to solve specific practical problems. E.g., designing a drug that blocks a viral enzyme based on basic research.
