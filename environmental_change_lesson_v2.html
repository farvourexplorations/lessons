Starter · 5 minutes

Look closely. What's changed?

Good geographers don't just look at a photo — they Notice, Think, and Wonder about it. This routine turns a simple photo into the start of an investigation.

Write down exactly what you can see in the photo — no guessing yet, just facts. Example: "The trees are gone. The ground is brown and bare. There are tyre tracks."

Use what you noticed to work out what change might be happening. Example: "I think this forest has been cut down, maybe to clear land for farming or building."

Turn your thinking into a genuine question — something you'd actually like to find out. Example: "Why is this forest being cleared, and what happens to the animals that lived there?"

Remember: good inquiry starts with a good question.

Skill focus

Good inquiry starts with a good question

Not all questions are equally useful for an inquiry. The strongest wonder-questions are open questions, not closed questions.

Usually start with Why, How, What if, or What might happen... They need explaining, and they lead to more questions. "Why is the river becoming polluted?"

Usually start with Is, Did, Are, Was, or Can. They can be answered in one word, and the inquiry stops there. "Is the river dirty?"
