The Big Question

Can maths be a language?

The sentence "I have three more apples than you" can be written in maths as a = y + 3. One is English. One is algebra. They mean exactly the same thing. Today you'll become fluent in both — and discover that algebra is just a shorthand for describing the world.

Here's the opening challenge: a parking garage charges $2 per hour plus a $5 flat fee. Without algebra, how would you calculate the cost for any number of hours? With algebra, you can write one expression — 5 + 2h — that answers the question for every possible value of h.

Variables are one of the most powerful inventions in all of human history. Instead of solving the same type of problem thousands of times, a variable lets you describe every case at once. That's not laziness — that's brilliance.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words algebraists use

Click each word to reveal its meaning. These are the precise terms mathematicians use to talk about expressions — and you'll use them too.

🔤 Variable 🔢 Coefficient ➕ Term 🔒 Constant ⬆️ Exponent
Variable — a letter that represents an unknown or changing number.

In 3x + 5, the variable is x. It can represent any number. When x = 2, the expression equals 11. When x = 10, it equals 35.

Your turn: in the expression 4y − 7, what is the variable? What is the expression's value when y = 3?
Coefficient — the number multiplied by a variable.

In 7x, the coefficient is 7. In ½y, the coefficient is ½. If there's no number written, the coefficient is 1 (so x means 1x).

Your turn: what is the coefficient of n in 9n? In n? In ¾n?
Term — a single number, variable, or product of numbers and variables.

In 5x + 3y − 8, there are 3 terms: 5x, 3y, and 8. Terms are separated by + and − signs.

Your turn: how many terms does 2a + b − 6c + 4 have?
Constant — a term that is just a number — no variable.

In 4x + 9, the constant is 9. It stays the same no matter what x is. That's why it's called a constant.

Your turn: in 2m + 3n − 7, what is the constant?
Exponent — tells you how many times to multiply a base by itself.

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. The base is 2, the exponent is 3. Read as "2 to the power of 3."

Your turn: what is 3⁴? What is 10²? What does 5¹ equal?

🔗 Combining Like Terms

LIKE TERMS HAVE THE SAME VARIABLE — THEY CAN BE ADDED TOGETHER

Like terms have the same variable part. You can combine them by adding their coefficients. 5x + 3x = 8x because you have 5 xs and 3 more xs = 8 xs total.

Real-World Connections

Expressions are everywhere 🌍

Every formula you'll ever use in science, finance, or engineering is an expression. The skill you've built today — writing and evaluating expressions — is the foundation of all of applied mathematics.

🏎️

Speed & Distance

Distance = speed × time, or d = rt. This is an expression with two variables. Substitute any speed and time to find the distance travelled.

💰

Earnings

If you earn $12 per hour plus a $20 bonus, your pay is 12h + 20. This expression gives your total for any number of hours h.

🖥️

Computer Memory

1 KB = 2¹⁰ bytes. 1 MB = 2²⁰. 1 GB = 2³⁰. Computers use powers of 2 to measure memory — exponents power every device you own.

🏠

Area & Perimeter

Area of a rectangle = lw. Perimeter = 2l + 2w = 2(l + w). These two are equivalent expressions — you can see the distributive property in action.
