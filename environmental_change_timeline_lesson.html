Main teaching

What is timeline evidence?

A single photo only shows one moment. Timeline evidence means looking at photos from different points in time so you can see a genuine pattern of change — and start to explain it.

1985
The Aral Sea — once one of the largest lakes in the world
2008
By 2008, the sea had shrunk to a fraction of its former size
Between 1985 and 2008, this place changed from a huge inland sea to a much smaller lake with dry, cracked seabed because the rivers that fed it were diverted for irrigation.
This is how a geographer models the pattern of change — always naming the two points in time, describing what's different, and suggesting a cause.
Skill focus

Gradual change or sudden change?

Not every environment changes at the same speed. Knowing which kind of change you're looking at helps you explain it properly.

Gradual change happens slowly, often over years or decades — like the Aral Sea shrinking, a city growing outward, or land being worn away by erosion. You usually need photos taken years apart to notice it clearly.

The Aral Sea example is gradual change — it took decades.

Sudden change happens fast — in days, hours, or even minutes — like a wildfire, flood, or landslide. Timeline evidence can be just as useful here, but the gap between photos is much shorter.

Before
A healthy forest — full canopy, layers of life
After
The same kind of forest after a wildfire
