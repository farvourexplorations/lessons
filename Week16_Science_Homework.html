Part A

🌋 Igneous rocks

Igneous rocks form when magma or lava cools down and turns solid. How fast it cools decides how big the crystals grow.

Granite — intrusive. Magma cooled slowly, deep underground. Large crystals.
Basalt — extrusive. Lava cooled quickly, at the surface. Tiny crystals.

Try the cooling simulation

Press the button and watch what happens to crystals as cooling speed changes.

Slow cooling

Deep underground · thousands of years

Large crystals → intrusive rock

Fast cooling

At the surface · minutes to days

Tiny crystals → extrusive rock

Fossils: mould or cast?

A mould is a hollow imprint left after the original plant or animal decays away. A cast forms when minerals later fill that hollow space and harden into a solid copy.

A fossil leaf — this is a mould, an imprint of the original leaf.

Part C continued

🔁 The rock cycle

Every rock on Earth is part of an endless cycle. Tap each stage on the diagram to read what happens there.

Rocks on the surface

Any rock at the surface is attacked by weathering, and the cycle starts again.

Sediment

Weathered fragments are eroded, transported, and deposited — often on a sea bed.

Sedimentary rock

Burial, compaction and cementation glue layers of sediment into solid rock.

Metamorphic rock

Deeper burial brings more heat and pressure, changing the rock — without melting it.

Magma

If burial continues, the rock eventually melts completely into liquid magma.

Igneous rock

As magma cools it solidifies into igneous rock, ready to be weathered all over again.

Part D

🌱 Soil

Soil forms when weathered rock mixes with decayed plants and animals. Tap each part of the diagram below.

1
2
3
4

1 — Mineral particles

Tiny weathered fragments of rock — sand, silt and clay.

2 — Water

Fills gaps between particles, carrying nutrients to plant roots.

3 — Air

Fills the remaining gaps — roots and soil organisms need this to breathe.

4 — Organic matter (humus)

Decayed plants and animals, packed full of nutrients for new plants.

Sandy soil

Large particles. Drains very fast.

Clay soil

Tiny particles. Drains very slowly. Hard when dry.

Loam soil

A balanced mix. Best for growing plants!

Try the sedimentation jar test

Soil and water, freshly shaken
