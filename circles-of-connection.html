The Big Question

What if technology had to be round to work?

This term you became quadrilateral detectives — sorting squares, rhombi, kites and trapezia by their straight sides and sharp corners. But look at the technology that actually connects communities around the world: wheels, satellite dishes, solar panels, drone rotors, irrigation systems. Almost none of it is a quadrilateral. Today we ask why.

Our inquiry question all term has been: how does technology help communities grow, stay healthy, learn, and connect with each other around the world? What if every part of the technology you invented to answer that question had to be a circle — not one straight edge allowed? What would you build, and why might round shapes work better than square ones for connecting people over distance?

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words circle detectives use

You already know parallel, bisect, perpendicular, isosceles, and line of symmetry from quadrilaterals. Circles need some new words. Tap each one below to reveal what it means.

Radius Diameter Circumference Chord Arc Sector Tangent
Radius — the distance from the centre of a circle to any point on its edge. Every radius in the same circle is exactly equal.
Diameter — a straight line passing through the centre, touching the edge on both sides. It's always exactly double the radius.
Circumference — the distance all the way around the outside of the circle. This is the circle's version of "perimeter." Circumference = π × diameter.
Chord — any straight line connecting two points on the circle's edge. The diameter is actually the longest possible chord.
Arc — a curved section of the circle's edge, like a slice of the circumference.
Sector — a "pizza slice" shaped region between two radii and the arc between them.
Tangent — a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point, and never crosses inside it.

🌊 Underwater city

Circular viewing domes and porthole windows resist water pressure evenly from every direction — a real reason engineers reach for circles underwater.

🏜️ Desert outpost

Circular solar collectors and dome shelters lose less heat and catch sunlight from every angle as the sun moves overhead.

☁️ Floating sky village

A circular rotor or balloon shape spreads lift evenly — no corner to catch the wind and tip the whole village over.

🧊 Polar research station

Round satellite dishes point toward orbiting satellites for communication, even in a landscape with no straight lines at all.
