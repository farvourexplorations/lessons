The Big Question

When do two stories agree?

You're thinking of two numbers. They add up to 20, and one is 4 more than the other. Each fact alone has infinitely many answers. Together, there's exactly one pair that works.

Every equation in two variables is a line of possible answers. Two lines usually cross at exactly one point. But what if the lines are parallel? Or lie right on top of each other? Then a system can have no answer, or infinitely many.

🧠 Warm-up — is the point a solution to BOTH equations?

A solution to a system must make every equation true at the same time.

✖️ System of Equations 🎯 Solution 🔄 Substitution ➖ Elimination 🛤️ Parallel Lines
System of Equations — two or more equations with the same variables, solved together.

x + y = 20 and x − y = 4
Solution — an ordered pair (x, y) that makes every equation true. On a graph it's where the lines cross.

(12, 8): 12 + 8 = 20 ✓ and 12 − 8 = 4 ✓
Substitution — solve one equation for a variable, then replace that variable in the other equation.

y = 2x, so x + 2x = 9 → x = 3
Elimination — add or subtract the equations so one variable cancels out.

(x + y = 20) + (x − y = 4) → 2x = 24
Parallel Lines — same slope, different y-intercepts. They never meet, so the system has no solution.

y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 3

Real-World Connections

Systems in the real world 🌍

Whenever two conditions must be true at once, there's a system hiding underneath.

🏪

Business

A company breaks even when income = costs. Selling past that point means profit.

🎟️

Ticket Sales

Knowing the number of tickets and the total money tells you exactly how many adult and child tickets were sold.

🧪

Mixtures

Chemists mix two solutions to get the exact strength they need: two unknowns, two equations.

🛰️

GPS

Your phone finds your location by solving a system of equations from several satellites.
