Meet the isomers

An isomer has exactly the same atoms as another molecule, just arranged differently. Different shape can mean very different properties!

Butane 正丁烷
Isobutane 异丁烷

Both molecules are C₄H₁₀ — same atoms, same formula. But the carbons connect in a different order. This is a structural isomer 结构异构体. Different structure changes properties, like boiling point.

Cis 顺式 — bent, usually liquid
Trans 反式 — straight, usually solid

Around a double bond, atoms can sit on the same side (cis, making a kink) or on opposite sides (trans, staying straight). Cis fats pack loosely and are usually liquid oils; trans fats pack tightly and are usually solid — and factory-made trans fats are linked to heart disease.

Smells like caraway 葛缕子

Enantiomers are mirror images of each other — like your left and right hand: same parts, opposite arrangement. It sounds like a tiny difference, but it matters: one mirror-image form of the molecule carvone smells like spearmint, while its mirror image smells completely different — like caraway seeds!

Cause and effect

One skeleton, two hormones

Testosterone and estradiol are both built on the exact same four-ring carbon skeleton called a steroid. Press the button to see the one small difference between them.

Testosterone 睾酮 (male hormone)
Estradiol 雌二醇 (female hormone)
Why this matters

Both molecules share the same carbon skeleton, but a small change in functional groups (and one ring becoming slightly different) is enough to give the body two completely different chemical messages. This shows how a tiny change to a molecule can lead to a huge change in what it does in a living body.

Real-life chemistry

A gas that ripens fruit

Ethylene is one of the simplest organic molecules — just two carbons and four hydrogens (C₂H₄). But plants use it as a hormone that tells fruit "start ripening now!"

One functional group, one big signal

Ethylene's simple structure is a carbon-carbon double bond — that's it. Yet this tiny molecule triggers a cascade of chemical changes inside fruit: starches turn into sugars, green chlorophyll breaks down, and the fruit softens. This is why grocery stores sometimes ripen fruit with ethylene gas on purpose!

Challenge

Mei and Tom's food label puzzle

Mei (美) and Tom are reading a food label. It lists both "unsaturated fat" (cis) and "trans fat."

"They must be the same thing — same atoms, same formula," says Mei.
"No, Mei — same atoms, but a different shape, so different effects on the body," says Tom.

Who is correct?

Tom is correct. Cis and trans fats are isomers — they have the exact same atoms and the same chemical formula, but the atoms are arranged differently around a double bond. That small structural difference changes the fat's shape, how it packs together, and how it affects the body. This is exactly why isomers matter so much in biology!
Remember

Same formula ≠ same molecule. Isomers prove that in biology, shape matters just as much as ingredients.
