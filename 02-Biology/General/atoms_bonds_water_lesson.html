How atoms connect

Three kinds of chemical bonds

A molecule forms when atoms connect using a bond. Tap each tab to compare the three main types.

Sharing electrons 共价键

A covalent bond forms when two atoms share electrons. It is a strong, stable bond. When millions of carbon atoms share electrons with their neighbours, they can build a giant, super-hard network.

Example: diamond — pure carbon, with every atom covalently bonded to four others.

A rough diamond — carbon atoms sharing electrons in a giant covalent network.

Transferring electrons 离子键

An ionic bond forms when one atom gives away an electron and another atom takes it. This leaves both atoms electrically charged — now called ions — and opposite charges attract, like tiny magnets.

Example: table salt (NaCl) — sodium gives an electron to chlorine, and Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions stack into a crystal.

Table salt crystals — held together by ionic bonds between Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions.

A weak but powerful attraction 氢键

A hydrogen bond is not a "real" bond inside one molecule — it is a weak attraction between molecules. Each water molecule is slightly positive near its hydrogen atoms and slightly negative near its oxygen atom, so water molecules pull on each other constantly.

Example: water droplets — millions of weak hydrogen bonds add up to give water its unusual properties.

Water beads into round droplets — hydrogen bonds pull water molecules toward each other.

Why this matters for life

Because ice floats, it forms an insulating layer on top of lakes and oceans in winter. The water underneath stays liquid, so fish and other organisms can survive the cold. If ice sank, most bodies of water would freeze solid from the bottom up!

Challenge

Mei and Tom's salad dressing puzzle

Mei (美) and Tom are making salad dressing. They shake olive oil and vinegar (mostly water) together hard, but a few seconds later the oil and water separate again into two layers.

"If I shake it hard enough, they will finally mix and stay mixed," says Mei.
"No, Mei — they will always separate again," says Tom.

Who is correct?

Tom is correct. Water is a polar molecule, so it forms hydrogen bonds with other water molecules. Oil is non-polar — it cannot form hydrogen bonds with water at all. Scientists sum this up as "like dissolves like": polar substances mix with polar substances, and non-polar substances mix with non-polar substances. No amount of shaking changes that chemistry!
Water: the solvent of life

Because water is polar, it can surround and dissolve many other polar or charged substances — like salts, sugars, and many proteins. This is why water is often called the "solvent of life" 生命的溶剂: almost every chemical reaction inside your cells happens dissolved in water.
