The Big Question

What lives below zero?

You've been counting since you were 3 years old — but always forward. Today we go backwards, past zero, into negative numbers. They're not just a maths trick — they describe the real world all around you.

It's −8°C in Winnipeg. A submarine dives to −200 metres. Your bank account shows −$15. These negatives aren't errors — they're precise mathematical descriptions of real quantities. The number line doesn't stop at zero.

🧠 Quick warm-up — what do these negative numbers describe?

Negative numbers were controversial for centuries. Ancient mathematicians called them "absurd" and "fictitious." It wasn't until the 1600s that they were fully accepted. Even smart people resist ideas that challenge what they already know.

➖ Negative Number 🔢 Rational Number 📏 Absolute Value ↔️ Opposite 🗺️ Quadrant
Negative Number — any number less than zero, written with a minus sign.

−5, −0.3, and −½ are all negative. On the number line, they sit to the left of zero (or below it on a vertical line).

Your turn: name three real-world situations where a negative number is the most natural way to describe something.
Rational Number — any number that can be written as a fraction p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

3, −½, 0.75, and −4.2 are all rational. They include all integers, fractions, and terminating or repeating decimals.

Your turn: is −6 a rational number? What about 0? What about 0.333...?
Absolute Value — the distance from zero on the number line, always positive.

|−7| = 7 and |7| = 7. Both are 7 steps from zero. Distance is never negative!

Your turn: what is |−15|? What is |0|? Can absolute value ever be negative?
Opposite — two numbers that are the same distance from zero but on different sides.

5 and −5 are opposites. −¾ and ¾ are opposites. The opposite of 0 is 0.

Your turn: what is the opposite of −12? Of 0.5? What is the opposite of the opposite of −3?
Quadrant — one of the four regions of the coordinate plane, created by the x and y axes crossing at the origin.

Quadrant I: (+,+) · Quadrant II: (−,+) · Quadrant III: (−,−) · Quadrant IV: (+,−)

Your turn: which quadrant is the point (−3, 5) in? What about (4, −2)?

Real-World Connections

Negative numbers are everywhere 🌍

Once you know how to think with negative numbers and coordinates, you see them in almost every field — from ocean science to game design to global finance.

🌊

Ocean Science

Sea level is defined as 0m. Ocean depths are negative: the Mariana Trench is −11,034m. Altitude above sea level is positive: Mount Everest is +8,849m.

🎮

Game Design

Every game map is a coordinate plane. Character positions, enemy locations, spawn points — all described as (x, y) pairs, often in all four quadrants.

📈

Finance

Stock prices move positively (gains) and negatively (losses). A net worth can be negative. Every financial statement uses positive and negative numbers.

🌐

GPS & Navigation

Latitude and longitude are a coordinate system. Negative latitude = South, negative longitude = West. Every GPS coordinate is a point in a global plane.
