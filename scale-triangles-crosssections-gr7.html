The Big Question

How can a whole house fit on one page?

A map of your city fits in your pocket. A floor plan of a mansion fits on a sheet of paper. The secret is a scale: every real length is multiplied by the same small number. Get the scale right and you can measure the real world from a drawing.

On a floor plan, 1 cm stands for 2 m. A bedroom is drawn 2 cm by 3 cm, so it's really 4 m by 6 m. Here's the puzzle: the drawing's area is 6 cm², but the real room is 24 m² — four times as many square units, not twice. Why?

🧠 Warm-up — can these sticks make a triangle?

Geometry is about what's possible: which drawings match reality, which shapes can exist, and what you'll see when you cut a solid open.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words geometers use

Click each word to reveal its meaning.

📏 Scale ✖️ Scale Factor 🔺 Triangle Inequality ☝️ Unique Triangle 🔪 Cross-Section
Scale — a ratio comparing a drawing length to the real length.

1 cm : 2 m means every 1 cm on paper is 2 m in real life.
Scale Factor — the number you multiply lengths by. Areas are multiplied by the scale factor squared.

Scale factor 2 → lengths ×2, area ×4.
Triangle Inequality — any two sides of a triangle must add up to MORE than the third side.

3, 4, 8: 3 + 4 = 7, which is less than 8 → no triangle!
Unique Triangle — conditions that allow only ONE triangle shape (and size).

Three side lengths → unique. Three angles → infinitely many (same shape, different sizes).
Cross-Section — the 2D shape you see when a plane slices through a 3D solid.

Slice a cylinder across → circle. Slice a square pyramid straight down through the top → triangle.

6
Real-World Connections

Geometry on the job 🌍

Scale, triangles and cross-sections are everyday tools for designers, builders and scientists.

🗺️

Maps

A scale bar like 1 cm : 5 km lets hikers measure trail distances with a ruler.

🌉

Bridges

Triangles are rigid: three fixed sides make one unique shape, so trusses don't wobble.

🏥

Medical Scans

CT and MRI scanners image the body as a stack of cross-sections, slice by slice.

🧱

Models

Model trains (1 : 87) and dollhouses (1 : 12) are scale drawings in 3D.
