Main teaching · Activity 1

Four types of environmental change

Every environmental change has a cause. Here are four types you'll see again and again.

🌳

Deforestation

Clearing forests, often for farming, building, or resources

🏭

Pollution

Harmful substances released into water, air, or land

🏙️

Urbanisation

Towns and cities growing as population increases

🌊

Climate-related

Flooding, coral bleaching, drought linked to a warming climate

Main teaching · Activity 2

Three real places, three real changes

In pairs, pick one case study. Explain it using: "In [place], the environment has changed because ___. This has caused ___."

Satellite view of deforestation spreading in a “fishbone” pattern along new roads, Amazon rainforest, Brazil
Cause

Forest is cleared for cattle farming and soy farming, and for roads that open up new land.

Impact

Habitat loss for wildlife, less rainfall in the region, and more carbon released into the air.

Evidence

Satellite images taken years apart show the forest shrinking and the “fishbone” road pattern spreading.

Bleached coral (front) next to healthy coral (back), Keppel Islands, Great Barrier Reef, Australia
Cause

Warmer sea temperatures stress the coral, causing it to lose the colourful algae living inside it.

Impact

Bleached coral turns white and can die, destroying homes for thousands of fish and sea creatures.

Evidence

Scientists compare reef photos over time and measure sea temperature to track bleaching events.

Ships stranded in the dried-up seabed, Moynaq, near the Aral Sea, Uzbekistan
Cause

The rivers that fed the Aral Sea were diverted to water cotton farms, so far less water reached the sea.

Impact

The sea shrank by around three-quarters, fishing towns lost their industry, and dust storms now carry salt and chemicals.

Evidence

Satellite images from the 1960s to today show the sea's outline shrinking year after year.

Check for understanding

Which case study is this?

“A sea shrank because the rivers that fed it were diverted to water cotton farms.”

Main teaching · Activity 1 · Modelled

Same flood risk, very different impact

Both these places face flooding — but one has far stronger defences than the other.

Place A
New Orleans, USA — flood damage after a levee (flood wall) failed in 2005
Place B
The Netherlands — the Delta Works, one of the world's largest flood-defence systems

Both places are low-lying and at risk of flooding — but years of investment in flood barriers means the Netherlands can hold back the same kind of flood that devastated New Orleans.
