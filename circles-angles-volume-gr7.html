The Big Question

Is a 16-inch pizza twice as big as an 8-inch pizza?

It sounds right — 16 is twice 8. But a 16-inch pizza actually has four times as much pizza. Circles hide a special number, π (pi), and once you understand it you'll never order pizza the same way again.

Wrap a string around any round object — a cup, a plate, a bike wheel. Then see how many times its diameter fits along that string. You'll always get a little more than 3. Every circle in the universe, same number. Why?

π Pi ⭕ Circumference ↔ Supplementary ✖ Vertical Angles 📦 Right Prism
Pi (π) — the ratio of ANY circle's circumference to its diameter: C ÷ d = π ≈ 3.14159…

It never ends and never repeats, so we use 3.14 or 22/7 as estimates.
Circumference — the distance around a circle. C = πd = 2πr.

A wheel with diameter 60 cm travels about 188 cm in one turn.
Supplementary / Complementary — two angles adding to 180° (a straight line) / 90° (a right angle).

130° and 50° are supplementary. 35° and 55° are complementary.
Vertical Angles — opposite angles made by two crossing lines. They are always equal.

Adjacent angles share a side and a vertex — like neighbours.
Right Prism — a solid with two identical parallel bases joined by rectangles.

Volume = Base area × height. Surface area = 2 × Base + Perimeter × height.

Real-World Connections

Geometry you can touch 🌍

Circles, angles and prisms show up in everything people design and build.

🍕

Pizza Value

Compare price per square inch: area grows with r², so bigger pizzas are often much better value.

🚲

Wheels

One turn of a wheel moves you one circumference. Odometers count turns × πd.

🛣️

Road Design

Engineers use supplementary and vertical angles to plan intersections and ramps.

📦

Packaging

Surface area = how much cardboard; volume = how much fits. Companies balance both.
