The Big Question

When are two shapes "the same"?

Two puzzle pieces fit the same hole if you can slide, turn or flip one onto the other. A photo and its enlargement aren't the same size, but they're clearly the same shape. Mathematicians make these ideas precise with transformations.

Video game designers move characters by adding to their coordinates. Artists flip images across a line. Phones zoom photos by multiplying. Each is a rule that moves every point. Which rules keep sizes and angles exactly the same?

🧠 Warm-up — is the image congruent (same size and shape)?

Rigid motions (translations, reflections, rotations) make congruent figures. Add a dilation and you get similar figures.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words for moving shapes

Click each word to reveal its meaning.

➡️ Translation 🪞 Reflection 🔄 Rotation 🔍 Dilation 🟰 Congruent vs Similar
Translation — slides every point the same distance in the same direction.

(x, y) → (x + 3, y − 2)
Reflection — flips a figure over a line, like a mirror.

Over the x-axis: (x, y) → (x, −y)
Rotation — turns a figure around a point.

90° counterclockwise about the origin: (x, y) → (−y, x)
Dilation — enlarges or shrinks a figure by a scale factor k from a centre.

From the origin: (x, y) → (kx, ky)
Congruent vs Similar — congruent figures match exactly after rigid motions. Similar figures match after rigid motions and a dilation: same angles, proportional sides.

Any two squares are similar.

📐 Angle Facts You Can Prove

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, translation copies one set of angles onto the other. That's why so many angles match. Triangles hide another rule: their angles always add to 180°.
