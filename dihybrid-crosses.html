🧬 The Concept — Worked Example

A dihybrid cross tracks how two different genes are inherited together. We'll use the classic example: pea plants, tracking seed shape and seed colour at the same time.

Allele Genotype Phenotype Homozygous / Heterozygous Dominant / Recessive
Allele — a different version of a gene. Here, R (round) and r (wrinkled) are alleles of the seed-shape gene; Y (yellow) and y (green) are alleles of the seed-colour gene.
Genotype — the combination of alleles an organism has, e.g. RrYy.
Phenotype — the physical characteristic that results from the genotype, e.g. "round, yellow seeds".
Homozygous — two identical alleles for a gene (e.g. RR or rr). Heterozygous — two different alleles for a gene (e.g. Rr).
Dominant allele (capital letter, e.g. R) shows up in the phenotype even if only one copy is present. Recessive allele (lowercase, e.g. r) only shows up in the phenotype if two copies are present.

1Start with true-breeding parents

A round-yellow-seeded pea plant (homozygous for both genes, RRYY) is crossed with a wrinkled-green-seeded pea plant (rryy). Each parent can only produce ONE type of gamete: RY and ry.

2All F1 offspring are identical hybrids

Every F1 offspring gets one full set of alleles from each parent: RY + ry → RrYy. All F1 plants are round, yellow-seeded — but heterozygous for both genes.

3Work out the gametes an F1 plant can make (the "FOIL" trick)

An RrYy plant makes 4 types of gamete. Pair each letter of the first gene with each letter of the second gene — First, Outer, Inner, Last:

RYRyrYry

4Cross two F1 plants (RrYy × RrYy) in a 4×4 Punnett square

Each parent's 4 gamete types go along the top and side of the grid. Every cell shows one possible offspring genotype — 16 combinations in total.

Round, Yellow
Round, Green
Wrinkled, Yellow
Wrinkled, Green
9 : 3 : 3 : 1

Round Yellow : Round Green : Wrinkled Yellow : Wrinkled Green — the classic dihybrid ratio, out of 16 offspring, whenever two heterozygous (RrYy) parents are crossed.
