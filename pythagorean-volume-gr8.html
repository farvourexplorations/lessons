The Big Question

How did builders make perfect corners without a protractor?

Ancient builders tied 12 equally spaced knots in a rope and pulled it into a triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5. The corner between the 3 and the 4 was always a perfect right angle. Why does 3-4-5 work, but 4-5-6 doesn't?

And here's another puzzle: an ice-cream cone and a cup with the same radius and height. How many cones of ice cream fill the cup? Most people guess 2. The real answer is a surprise.

🧠 Warm-up — do these side lengths make a right triangle?

In a right triangle, the square on the longest side equals the two smaller squares added together.

📐 Legs & Hypotenuse 🟥 Pythagorean Theorem 🔁 Converse 📍 Distance Formula 🥤 Volume
Legs & Hypotenuse — the legs (a, b) form the right angle. The hypotenuse (c) is opposite it and is always the longest side.

In 3-4-5, the hypotenuse is 5.
Pythagorean Theorem — in any right triangle, a² + b² = c².

3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²
Converse — if a² + b² = c² for a triangle's sides, then the triangle IS a right triangle. If a² + b² > c² it's acute; if < c² it's obtuse.

4-5-6: 16 + 25 = 41 > 36 → acute
Distance between points — draw a right triangle with horizontal and vertical legs, then use a² + b² = c².

(1, 2) to (4, 6): legs 3 and 4 → distance 5
Volume — the space inside a solid. Cylinder πr²h · Cone ⅓πr²h · Sphere ⁴⁄₃πr³.

A cone holds exactly ⅓ of a cylinder with the same r and h.

🥤 Cylinders, Cones & Spheres

A cylinder's volume is base area × height: πr²h. A cone with the same base and height holds exactly one third as much. A sphere fits inside a cylinder of height 2r and fills two thirds of it.

6
Real-World Connections

Pythagoras and volume in the real world 🌍

These ideas help people build, navigate and design every day.

🪜

Ladders

Firefighters use a² + b² = c² to know how far up a wall a ladder will reach.

📺

Screens

A "55-inch" TV is measured along its diagonal, the hypotenuse of the screen.

🛢️

Tanks

Engineers use πr²h to work out how much water or fuel a cylindrical tank holds.

🌍

Planets

Scientists estimate a planet's volume with ⁴⁄₃πr³ to work out its density.
