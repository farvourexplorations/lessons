Reading text · How We Affect Our Planet

Pollution

Every day, human activities change the world around us. Some of these changes help us live better lives, but many of them harm the environment. Understanding these impacts is the first step to making better choices.

Pollution is one of the biggest problems. When we burn fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas in cars, factories, and power plants, we release harmful gases into the air. These gases trap heat in the atmosphere, causing global warming. This leads to melting ice caps, rising sea levels, and more extreme weather like floods and droughts.

Burning fossil fuels in factories and power plants releases harmful gases into the air.

Deforestation

Deforestation is another major issue. Every year, millions of trees are cut down to make room for farms, roads, and cities. Trees absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen, so when we remove them, more greenhouse gases stay in the air. Deforestation also destroys the habitats of countless animals and plants, pushing many species toward extinction.

Millions of trees are cut down every year to make room for farms, roads, and cities.

Why does removing trees matter?

🌳🌳🌳

A healthy forest

Trees absorb CO₂ from the air and release oxygen (O₂). Greenhouse gases stay low. Animals and plants have homes.

🌳🪓🏚️

After deforestation

Fewer trees means less CO₂ is absorbed. Greenhouse gases build up in the air. Habitats and species are lost.

Our waste

Our waste also causes serious problems. Plastic bags, bottles, and packaging take hundreds of years to break down. Much of this plastic ends up in our oceans, where it harms marine life. Sea turtles mistake plastic bags for jellyfish, and fish eat tiny plastic particles that eventually enter our food chain.

Floating in the water, a plastic bag can look just like a jellyfish to a hungry sea turtle.

Agriculture and farming

Agriculture and farming also impact the environment. Farmers use chemical fertilizers and pesticides to grow more food, but these chemicals can wash into rivers and lakes. This causes water pollution and harms aquatic life. Raising livestock, especially cows, releases large amounts of methane — a powerful greenhouse gas.

Chemical fertilizers and pesticides help crops grow, but can wash into rivers and lakes.

Urbanization

Urbanization, or the growth of cities, also changes the environment. As we build more homes, roads, and shopping centres, we cover natural land with concrete and asphalt. This reduces the amount of land that can absorb rainwater and increases the risk of flooding.

Concrete and asphalt cover natural land as cities grow.

Overconsumption — and solutions

Overconsumption means using more resources than the Earth can replace. We use too much water, energy, wood, and minerals. If we continue at this rate, future generations will not have enough resources to meet their needs.

Fortunately, there are solutions. We can reduce, reuse, and recycle to cut down on waste. We can use renewable energy sources like solar, wind, and water power instead of fossil fuels. We can plant more trees and protect existing forests. We can choose to walk, cycle, or use public transport to reduce air pollution.

Every small action makes a difference. By understanding how our activities impact the environment, we can make choices that protect our planet for ourselves and future generations.

Solar and wind power are renewable alternatives to fossil fuels.

Grammar Practice · Conditionals

Zero & First Conditional

For general truths and facts.

Structure

If + present simple, … present simple

Example: If you heat water, it boils.

📓 Practice — write the completed sentences in your notebook
1

If you cut down trees, you ____________ (destroy) animal habitats.

2

If you burn fossil fuels, you ____________ (release) greenhouse gases.

3

If you recycle plastic, you ____________ (reduce) waste in the ocean.

For real future possibilities — things likely to happen.

Structure

If + present simple, … will + base verb

Example: If we use more solar energy, we will reduce air pollution.

📓 Practice — write the completed sentences in your notebook
4

If we ____________ (plant) more trees, we ____________ (absorb) more carbon dioxide.

5

If people ____________ (walk) more, the air ____________ (become) cleaner.

6

If the government ____________ (ban) plastic bags, the ocean ____________ (have) less plastic waste.

Grammar Practice · Conditionals

Second & Third Conditional

For imaginary or unlikely situations in the present.

Structure

If + past simple, … would + base verb

Example: If I were the mayor, I would build more parks.

📓 Practice — write the completed sentences in your notebook
7

If I ____________ (have) a lot of money, I ____________ (buy) an electric car.

8

If every family ____________ (use) less water, we ____________ (save) thousands of litres a day.

9

If the government ____________ (ban) all fossil fuels tomorrow, life ____________ (be) very different.

For imagining how the past could have been different.

Structure

If + past perfect, … would have + past participle

Example: If we had recycled more, we would have produced less waste.

📓 Practice — write the completed sentences in your notebook
10

If we ____________ (know) about climate change earlier, we ____________ (take) action sooner.

11

If the factory ____________ (not release) chemicals into the river, the fish ____________ (not die).

12

If people ____________ (use) fewer plastic bags in the past, the oceans ____________ (become) cleaner today.
