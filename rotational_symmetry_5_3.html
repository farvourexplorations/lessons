Key idea

Turn it — does it still look the same?

A shape has rotational symmetry if it can be turned about a point and still look exactly the same, before it gets back to the start.

Order

The order of rotational symmetry is the number of times a shape looks the same during one full turn (360°).

Order 2

Order 4

Worked example

Using tracing paper to find the order

This method works for any shape or pattern.

Trace the shape onto tracing paper.
Put your pencil on the centre of the shape, through both papers.
Turn the tracing paper one full turn and count how many times the shape fits exactly onto itself.
Tip

If your shape doesn't fit exactly onto itself, your pencil wasn't on the exact centre — try again with a different centre point.

Example: parallelogram vs trapezium

A parallelogram looks the same after a half-turn and a full turn — rotational symmetry order 2.

An isosceles trapezium only looks the same after a full turn — rotational symmetry order 1 (no rotational symmetry).

Going further

Joining shapes can change the order

When you join tiles or shapes together to make a pattern, the order of rotational symmetry of the whole pattern can be different from a single tile.

One tile

A single flower tile has rotational symmetry order 4 on its own.

Two tiles joined

Join two of the same tile in a row and the pattern's order can drop to order 2 — always check the whole pattern, not just one tile.

Always trace the whole pattern, not just one part of it.
