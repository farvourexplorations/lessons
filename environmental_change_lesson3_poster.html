Quick recap: what makes a question strong?

In Lesson 1 we learned that open questions — the ones that start with Why, How, What if... — make the best inquiry questions, because they need real explanation instead of a one-word answer.

Activity 1 · Make your poster

Put your inquiry question on an A3 poster

Use your A3 paper to make a clear, readable poster with these four parts. Big writing — you'll display it for others to read.

MY INQUIRY QUESTION POSTER
🌍 My Environmental Change
❓ My Inquiry Question
🔍 I want to find out...
💡 I think this question is important because...
A3 paper Markers or pens
Activity 2 · Gallery walk

Walk, read, and spot great questions

Display your poster around the room. Walk around quietly and read your classmates' questions. Look for questions that are:

⭐

Interesting

It makes you want to know more

⭐

Important

It matters for people, animals, or places

⭐

Researchable

You could actually find an answer to it

Activity 3 · Peer feedback & revise

Give feedback, then improve your question

Leave kind, useful feedback on a sticky note or by talking to the poster's owner. Use these sentence starters:

"I like..."

Say what works well about their question.

"I wonder..."

Share a genuine thought their question gave you.

"Maybe you could ask..."

Suggest one way to make it stronger or more open.

Now revise

Read the feedback you received, then update your poster with a stronger, final version of your inquiry question.
