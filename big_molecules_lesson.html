Build (and break) a polymer

A polymer is a long chain built from small repeating units called monomers. Link monomers together, or break the chain apart, and watch what happens to water.

Supply
Polymer chain
Two opposite reactions

Dehydration synthesis 脱水合成: joining two monomers removes a water molecule.

Hydrolysis 水解: breaking a polymer apart adds a water molecule back. This is exactly how your digestive system breaks down food!

Carbohydrates

Sugars, big and small

A carbohydrate is built from sugar units. Tap each tab to see the three sizes.

One sugar unit 单糖

The simplest carbohydrate — just one sugar ring. Glucose is the most important monosaccharide: it's the sugar your cells burn for energy. You'll find it naturally in sweet fruit like grapes.

Grapes are naturally rich in glucose and fructose — simple monosaccharides.

Two sugars joined 双糖

Two monosaccharides linked by dehydration synthesis. Sucrose (table sugar) = glucose + fructose joined together. Your body must hydrolyze it back into single sugars before your cells can use it.

Sugar cubes are almost pure sucrose — a disaccharide.

Many sugars in a chain 多糖

Hundreds or thousands of monosaccharides linked together. Plants store energy as starch; animals store energy as glycogen; plants build cell walls from cellulose; insects build hard shells from chitin.

Rice is mostly starch — a polysaccharide built entirely from glucose units.
Lipids

Fats, membranes, and hormones

A lipid does not dissolve in water. Unlike carbs and proteins, lipids are not true polymers — they aren't built from one repeating monomer.

Energy storage 脂肪

A fat is built from one glycerol molecule joined to three fatty acid chains — that's why it's also called a triglyceride. Fats pack a huge amount of energy into a small space and cushion your organs.

Olive oil is a liquid fat — a triglyceride rich in unsaturated (cis) fatty acids.

The molecule that builds membranes 磷脂

A phospholipid looks like a fat, but one fatty acid tail is swapped for a phosphate group. This gives it a water-loving "head" and water-fearing "tails" — press the button to see what happens when it meets water.

Heads (purple) face outward toward water; tails (orange) hide inside — this exact structure builds every cell membrane.

Four fused rings 类固醇

A steroid is built from four fused carbon rings. Remember testosterone and estradiol from the last lesson? Both are steroids! Cholesterol is another steroid — it helps build cell membranes and is the starting material your body uses to build hormones.

Proteins

Four levels of folding

A protein is a chain of amino acids that folds into a precise 3D shape. Tap each level to watch it fold, step by step.

Primary structure

Cause and effect

What happens when you cook an egg?

Heat, acid, or extreme pH can break the weak bonds holding a protein's 3D shape together. This is called denaturation.

Unfolding, not disappearing

Raw egg white is clear and runny because its proteins are folded into their normal 3D shape. Heat breaks the weak bonds holding that shape together, so the proteins unfold and tangle up with their neighbours — turning the egg white solid and opaque. The atoms are still all there, but the protein's shape (and its function) is gone for good.

Denaturation is usually permanent
Nucleic acids

The molecule of instructions

A nucleotide has three parts: a phosphate group, a sugar, and a nitrogenous base.

Phosphate 磷酸基 — Sugar — Base

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) 脱氧核糖核酸 uses the sugar deoxyribose and the base thymine (T). Two DNA strands twist together into a double helix.

RNA (ribonucleic acid) 核糖核酸 uses the sugar ribose and the base uracil (U) instead of thymine. RNA is usually a single strand.

Challenge

Mei and Tom's lipid puzzle

Mei (美) and Tom are reviewing for a test. Mei says fat molecules are polymers, just like starch, because they're both big molecules made of repeating parts.

"A fat is a polymer — it's a big molecule made of repeating parts, just like starch," says Mei.
"No, Mei — a fat is big, but it isn't a true polymer," says Tom.

Who is correct?

Tom is correct. A true polymer is built from one repeating monomer, like glucose repeating in starch or amino acids repeating in a protein. A fat is built from one glycerol molecule joined to three different fatty acid chains — not one repeating unit. So fats are large molecules, but biologists don't call them true polymers!
Remember

Big ≠ polymer. A true polymer repeats one type of monomer over and over — carbohydrates, proteins, and nucleic acids all qualify, but lipids do not.
