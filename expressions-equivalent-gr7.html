The Big Question

Can two expressions look different but always be equal?

A garden is x metres long. Ana writes the fence length as x + x + 4 + 4. Ben writes 2x + 8. Cara writes 2(x + 4). They're all right! These are equivalent expressions — equal for every value of x.

A store says "20% off". Your friend says "just pay 80%". Is the price p − 0.2p really the same as 0.8p? And which way of writing it is more useful at the checkout?

🧠 Warm-up — equivalent or not?

Expressions are like outfits: the same number can wear a combined look, an expanded look, or a factored look. Each one shows you something different about the situation.

🧱 Like Terms 🔢 Coefficient 🔀 Expand 📦 Factor 🎭 Equivalent
Like Terms — terms with exactly the same variable part. You can combine them.

3x and −5x are like terms (3x − 5x = −2x). 3x and 3 are NOT.
Coefficient — the number multiplying a variable. It can be negative, a fraction or a decimal.

In −½x + 0.4y, the coefficients are −½ and 0.4.
Expand — use the distributive property to remove brackets.

−3(2x − 5) = −6x + 15
Factor — the reverse of expanding: pull out a common factor.

12x + 18 = 6(2x + 3)
Equivalent Expressions — expressions that are equal for every value of the variable.

a + 0.05a and 1.05a are equivalent: test any a and they match.

Real-World Connections

Expressions at work 🌍

Choosing the right form of an expression saves time and reveals meaning.

🛒

Shopping

Cashiers multiply by 0.85 for 15% off instead of finding 15% and subtracting — same result, one step.

💻

Spreadsheets

A formula like =1.08*B2 adds 8% tax to a whole column at once.

🏗️

Construction

Perimeter 2(l + w) or 2l + 2w — builders pick whichever form fits the measurements they have.

🎟️

Group Tickets

5 friends each pay (t + 3): factored shows per-person cost, expanded 5t + 15 shows the total and fees.
