The Big Question

What makes each quadrilateral special?

Quadrilateral tiles are used on kitchen and bathroom walls and floors because they fit together exactly, leaving no gaps. But not every four-sided shape is the same — a square, a kite and a trapezium all behave differently. To order the right tiles, you need to know exactly what makes each one tick.

Here's the big secret: there are seven special quadrilaterals — square, rectangle, parallelogram, rhombus, trapezium, isosceles trapezium and kite. Every one of them is built from the same four ingredients: sides, angles, diagonals and lines of symmetry. Today you'll learn to identify, describe, classify and sketch all seven.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words quadrilateral detectives use

Click each word to reveal its meaning. You'll use every one of these words in today's investigations.

▱ Quadrilateral ╳ Diagonal ∥ Parallel ✂ Bisect 🔧 Decompose 🧩 Tessellate
Quadrilateral — any closed 2D shape with exactly four straight sides.

Your turn: name three quadrilaterals you can see around you right now.
Diagonal — a straight line joining two opposite corners of a shape (not along an edge). Every quadrilateral has two diagonals.

Your turn: in a square, do the diagonals look longer or shorter than the sides?
Parallel — two lines that always stay the same distance apart and never meet, however far they are extended. Shown with matching arrow symbols (→ →).

Your turn: find a pair of parallel lines in your classroom.
Bisect — to cut something exactly in half. "The diagonals bisect each other" means each diagonal cuts the other into two equal parts.

Your turn: if a 10 cm diagonal is bisected, how long is each half?
Decompose — to break a shape apart into smaller shapes. A square can be decomposed into two triangles, for example.

Your turn: how else could you decompose a square?
Tessellate — shapes tessellate when copies of them fit together with no gaps and no overlaps, like tiles on a floor.

Your turn: predict — do you think every quadrilateral can tessellate?

Real-World Connections

Quadrilaterals are everywhere 🌍

Once you can name and describe quadrilaterals, you'll start spotting them everywhere — in floors, windows, kites, and even road signs.

🚿

Tiling

Bathroom and kitchen tiles are almost always quadrilaterals, because they tessellate — they fit together with no gaps, so no material is wasted.

🏠

Architecture

Roof trusses often use parallelograms and trapeziums for strength, while diamond-shaped (rhombus) windows add style to a building's design.

🎨

Art & Pattern

Artists like M.C. Escher built entire pictures from tessellating quadrilaterals, proving that maths and art share the same rules.

🪁

Kite Flying

A flying kite is shaped like the quadrilateral of the same name — its line of symmetry keeps it balanced and stable in the wind.
