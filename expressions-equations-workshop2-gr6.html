The Big Question

What makes an equation true?

An expression says something. An equation says two things are equal. The moment you add an equals sign, a whole new question appears: what value makes this true? That question is the heart of all algebra.

A bag of oranges costs $1.50 per orange plus a $2 bag fee. You spend exactly $14. How many oranges did you buy? You just described an equation: 1.5n + 2 = 14. Today you'll learn to solve it — and explain why your answer is the only one that works.

🧠 Warm-up — is x = 4 a solution to each equation?

Solving an equation is like being a detective. You have a clue (the equation), a suspect (the variable), and your job is to find the one value that makes the equation balance perfectly. Every step you take must keep both sides equal.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words equation-solvers use

Click each word to reveal its meaning. These are the tools you'll use throughout Workshop 2.

Equation — a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal, shown with an = sign.

x + 5 = 12 is an equation. It asks: what value of x makes both sides equal? Answer: x = 7.

Your turn: write an equation that has the solution x = 10.
Solution — the value of the variable that makes the equation true.

For x + 5 = 12, the solution is x = 7. You can verify: 7 + 5 = 12 ✓

Your turn: verify that x = 3 is the solution to 4x = 12 by substituting.
Inequality — a statement that one quantity is greater than, less than, or not equal to another.

x > 5 means x can be any number greater than 5 — infinitely many solutions! That's what makes inequalities different from equations.

Your turn: list 5 solutions to the inequality x < 10.
Independent Variable — the variable you control or choose. It's the input.

In d = 60t (distance = speed × time), time t is the independent variable — you choose how long you drive.

Your turn: in a recipe, if cost depends on how many cupcakes you make, which is the independent variable?
Dependent Variable — the variable whose value depends on the independent variable. It's the output.

In d = 60t, distance d is the dependent variable — it depends on how long you drive.

Your turn: in y = 3x + 1, which is the independent variable and which is dependent?

🔑 The key difference between equations and inequalities:

x + 3 = 8
Equation — exactly ONE solution (x = 5)
x + 3 > 8
Inequality — INFINITE solutions (x > 5)

🧮 Solving One-Step Equations

To solve x + p = q or px = q, use inverse operations — whatever was done to x, you undo it. Add? Subtract to undo. Multiply? Divide to undo. The golden rule: whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Real-World Connections

Equations are everywhere 🌍

Every time someone calculates a fair split, works out a budget, checks a speed limit, or plans a journey — they're using the ideas you learned today. Equations and inequalities are the grammar of problem-solving.

⚖️

Law & Justice

Sentencing guidelines use inequalities: "a fine of at least $500" is f ≥ 500. Speed limits are inequalities: v ≤ 60 mph. The law is written in the language of inequalities.

🏗️

Engineering

Engineers solve equations constantly — load = strength × safety_factor. Every bridge, building, and machine was designed by solving equations like the ones you practised today.

💊

Medicine

Dose = weight × rate_per_kg. A doctor solving for dose is solving an equation. Getting it wrong by even a small amount can be dangerous. Algebra saves lives.

🎯

Sports Analytics

Points per game, win rates, scoring inequalities — every sports analyst writes equations and inequalities to predict performance and evaluate players.
