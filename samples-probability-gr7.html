The Big Question

Can a small group tell you about everyone?

Your school has 500 students. You want to know their favourite lunch, but you can't ask everyone. If you ask 40 students, can you trust the answer? It depends completely on how you choose them.

Roll two dice and add them. Is every total from 2 to 12 equally likely? Most people guess yes. But roll them 100 times and 7 shows up about six times as often as 2. By the end of today you'll know exactly why.

🧠 Warm-up — fair sample or biased?

Statistics and probability are about uncertainty: making good decisions when you can't know everything for sure.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words data scientists use

Click each word to reveal its meaning.

👥 Population & Sample 🎯 Random Sample ⚠️ Bias 📏 MAD 🎲 Probability
Population & Sample — the population is the whole group you want to know about; a sample is the part you actually measure.

Population: all 500 students. Sample: the 40 you survey.
Random Sample — every member of the population has an equal chance of being chosen. Random samples tend to represent the population.

Drawing 40 names from a hat of all 500 students.
Bias — a sampling method that favours some results over others.

Asking only the basketball team about favourite sports is biased.
Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) — the average distance of the data from the mean. Used to measure how far apart two groups really are.

If two means differ by 3 MADs, the groups are clearly different.
Probability — a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain) for how likely an event is. Theoretical = what should happen; experimental = what did happen.

P(heads) = ½ in theory; you might get 48 heads in 100 flips.

Investigation 2 · 7.SP.B.3–4

⚖️ Are Two Groups Really Different?

Two groups can have different means just by chance. To judge, compare the difference in means to the spread (MAD). A difference of 2 or more MADs is a strong sign the groups really differ.

Real-World Connections

Data and chance in the real world 🌍

Every poll, weather forecast and medical trial depends on the ideas from today.

🗳️

Election Polls

Pollsters randomly sample about 1,000 voters. A random sample that size usually lands within a few percent of the truth.

💊

Medical Trials

Researchers compare a treatment group to a control group. If the difference is many MADs, the medicine probably works.

🌦️

Weather

"70% chance of rain" comes from simulations of many possible tomorrows.

🏭

Quality Control

Factories test a random sample of products to estimate how many in a batch are faulty.
