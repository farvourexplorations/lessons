4.4 · Begin Planning a Longer Story

📖 What makes a chapter?

Longer stories need to be planned carefully, especially once they're divided into chapters. Tap each card to reveal the idea.

Why chapters? Starting a new chapter Chapters = mini stories Ending on a cliffhanger
Chapters organise a longer story into episodes. Instead of one giant block of text, a story is broken into manageable sections — like episodes of a TV series — so the reader can follow the journey one step at a time.
A new chapter usually starts: after the end of a series of events, or when there's a change of scene — much like when a play changes to a new setting. It signals to the reader: "something is shifting now."
Each chapter works like a mini story: the beginning sets the scene and grabs attention, the middle describes the action and develops the storyline, and the ending wraps up that episode.
Chapters often end on a climax or leave the reader wondering about something — a cliffhanger. That's the trick that makes a reader turn the page instead of putting the book down.

🧩 Writing techniques toolkit

TAP EACH TECHNIQUE TO SEE HOW IT'S USED
Ellipsis (...) Italics Brackets
Ellipsis ( ... ) shows a pause, hesitation, or something trailing off — great for building suspense at the end of a chapter. Example: "The door creaked open... and something was waiting."
Italics add emphasis to a word or show a character's inner thought. Example: I have no idea whether that life is large or small.
Brackets add extra information without interrupting the main sentence. Example: The rocket (built over three years) finally launched.

📋 Chapter-by-chapter plan

USE THIS PATTERN AS A GUIDE, THEN JOT YOUR OWN IDEAS BELOW EACH ONE
Chapter 1 — Introduce
Introduce your characters and what they're like. Give clues about why they're travelling. Mention the special item that will run through the story.
Chapter 2 — Settle In
Describe the new setting or scenery. Show the special item being used or mentioned again.
Chapter 3 — Build the Problem
A problem or challenge involving the special item or setting builds toward a climax.
Chapter 4 — Resolve
The special item helps resolve the problem. Wrap up the episode.

4.5 · Going Back and Looking Into the Future

🛸 What counts as science fiction?

Science fiction imagines worlds shaped by science, technology, space, or time — often set in the future or on other worlds. It's different from fantasy, which usually relies on magic instead of science or invented technology.

⏳ Travelling in a time machine

Almost always science fiction — it imagines technology that lets people move through time, and often explores the consequences of changing the past or future.

🪐 A story set in the year 2075

Usually science fiction if it imagines how technology or society might change — but a story could also just be "set in the future" without exploring any science-fiction ideas at all. Context matters.

🕵️ A secret agent with impossible gadgets

This could sit between spy fiction and science fiction — it becomes science fiction when the gadgets are imagined as real (if unlikely) future technology, rather than just fantastical for fun.

🌊 Discovering a lost city under the sea

This could be adventure fiction, fantasy, or science fiction depending on how it's explained — is the lost city explained by advanced (if lost) technology, or by magic?

4.5 · The Diary of a Space Traveller

📔 A real science-fiction extract

Your workbook contains a full extract from a classic 20th-century Indian science-fiction book. Read the whole extract there — here's the background you need before you dive in.

🎬

Satyajit Ray (1921–1992) was an Indian filmmaker, screenwriter, music composer, graphic artist, lyricist and author, born in Kolkata into a family deeply involved in the arts. One of his most famous characters was Professor Shonku, a fictional scientist starring in a series of Bengali science-fiction books published from 1965 onward. The professor's full name is Professor Trilokeshwar Shonku.

📖 What happens in the extract (in your workbook)

Professor Shonku is flying towards Mars in his spaceship with his assistant Prahlad and his robot companion, Bidhushekhar. Shonku writes diary entries describing the journey — what they're packing, his hopes about alien life, and a moment when Bidhushekhar suddenly seizes the controls and has to be switched off. After landing, Shonku describes a Mars where the colours are reversed from Earth: blue plants instead of green, a green sky, and rivers that look like red jelly. No living creature has appeared yet, and the silence feels strange and unsettling.
