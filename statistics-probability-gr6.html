The Big Question

What can numbers tell us that words can't?

Imagine you want to know whether students in your school get enough sleep. You could ask one person — but that's just one story. Statistics lets you ask everyone, find the pattern, and say something true about the whole group.

Here's a dataset of hours of sleep 10 students got last night: 7, 6, 8, 5, 9, 7, 6, 8, 7, 5. What can you say about this group? Is it typical? Spread out? What's the most common value? By the end of today, you'll be able to answer all of those questions — precisely.

Discover the Vocabulary

📚 Words statisticians use

Click each word to reveal its meaning. These are the precise terms you'll use all the way through middle and high school statistics.

📐 Mean ⬛ Median 🔁 Mode 📏 IQR 📉 MAD
Mean — the average. Add all values and divide by how many there are.

Data: 3, 7, 5, 8, 2 → Sum = 25 → Mean = 25 ÷ 5 = 5

Your turn: find the mean of 10, 4, 8, 6, 7.
Median — the middle value when data is sorted in order.

Sorted: 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 → Median = 5. For even counts, average the two middle values.

Your turn: find the median of 4, 9, 2, 7, 1, 6.
Mode — the value that appears most often. A dataset can have no mode, one mode, or multiple modes.

Data: 3, 7, 3, 9, 3, 7 → Mode = 3 (appears 3 times)

Your turn: what is the mode of 5, 2, 8, 2, 9, 5, 2?
Interquartile Range (IQR) — the range of the middle 50% of the data. IQR = Q3 − Q1.

It measures spread while ignoring extreme outliers. A small IQR means the data clusters tightly; a large IQR means it's spread out.

Your turn: data sorted: 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13. Q1 = 4, Q3 = 11. IQR = ?
Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) — the average distance each data point is from the mean.

Step 1: find the mean. Step 2: find each value's distance from the mean. Step 3: average those distances.

Your turn: data: 2, 4, 6, 8. Mean = 5. Distances: 3, 1, 1, 3. MAD = (3+1+1+3)÷4 = 2.

❓ What Makes a Question Statistical?

A statistical question anticipates variability — it expects different answers from different people or things. "How tall are you?" has one answer. "How tall are students in this school?" expects many different answers. That difference is everything.

📊 Displaying Data — Dot Plots, Histograms & Box Plots

The same data can be displayed three different ways — and each display reveals something different. Predict before you plot!

📝 Before you start — predict:

📍 Dot Plot

EACH DOT = ONE DATA POINT · STACKED ABOVE THE NUMBER LINE · GREAT FOR SMALL DATASETS

📊 Histogram

GROUPS DATA INTO INTERVALS (BINS) · SHOWS DISTRIBUTION SHAPE · GREAT FOR LARGER DATASETS

📦 Box Plot (Box-and-Whisker)

SHOWS MIN, Q1, MEDIAN, Q3, MAX · REVEALS SPREAD AND SYMMETRY AT A GLANCE

Real-World Connections

Statistics is everywhere 🌍

Every decision made with data uses these tools. From the weather forecast to your favourite sports team's draft picks, statistics shapes the world you live in.

🏥

Medicine

Clinical trials collect data on thousands of patients, calculate means and medians, and display distributions to determine whether a drug actually works — or if the result was just chance.

⚽

Sports Analytics

Teams use MAD and IQR to evaluate consistency. A player with a low MAD in scoring is more reliable than one with a high mean but high variability.

🌦️

Climate Science

Climatologists use histograms and box plots to compare temperature distributions across decades. The shift in the median tells the story of climate change.

📱

Tech & Social Media

Every recommendation algorithm uses statistics. The mean time spent per video, the median engagement rate, the IQR of user behaviour — all drive what you see next.
