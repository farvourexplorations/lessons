Phase 1 · Wonder

🍽️ What Is Your Food Actually Made Of?

Bread, chicken, and butter all look and taste completely different. But underneath, they're all built from a surprisingly small toolkit of chemical building blocks — and biologists have simple chemical tests that can reveal exactly which ones are hiding inside any food.

Imagine you're a food scientist handed three unlabelled white powders. You can't taste or smell them (lab safety rule!). How could you use a small number of chemical tests to work out which one is a starch, which is a protein, and which is a sugar — without ever seeing what they're made from?

✏️ Your Thinking — Before We Begin

Every carbohydrate, fat and protein is built from smaller repeating units, and five colour-changing chemical tests can reveal exactly which molecules are present in any food sample. Even the DNA in every one of your cells follows a beautifully simple building code — just two rules.

Phase 2 · 4.1 Elements and Building Blocks

🔗 Elements and Building Blocks

All biological molecules are built from a small set of chemical elements, and large molecules are chains of smaller repeating units joined together.

🧪 Chemical Elements — Tap Each

Carbohydrates Fats and Oils Proteins
🍞 Carbohydrates are made of the elements carbon (C), hydrogen (H) and oxygen (O).
🧈 Fats and oils are made of the elements carbon (C), hydrogen (H) and oxygen (O) — the same three elements as carbohydrates.
🥩 Proteins are made of carbon (C), hydrogen (H), oxygen (O) and nitrogen (N). Exam trap: nitrogen is the element that distinguishes proteins from carbohydrates and fats.

🧱 Large Molecules Are Built From Smaller Molecules

Large biological molecules aren't single units — they are long chains made of many smaller "building block" molecules joined together.

🌾 Starch

Built from many glucose molecules joined together in a chain. Used by plants to store energy.

🍖 Glycogen

Built from many glucose molecules joined together in a (highly branched) chain. Used by animals to store energy, mainly in the liver and muscles.

🌿 Cellulose

Built from many glucose molecules joined together in long, strong chains. Makes up plant cell walls, giving them strength and support.

🥚 Proteins

Built from many amino acids joined together in a chain. Used to build structures (like muscle) and to make enzymes and antibodies.

🧈 Fats and Oils

Each fat/oil molecule is built from one glycerol molecule joined to (usually three) fatty acid molecules. Used for long-term energy storage and insulation.

🟤 Iodine Solution Test (Starch)

Add a few drops of iodine solution (orange-brown) directly to the food sample.

✅ Positive: colour changes to blue-black.
❌ Negative: stays orange-brown.

🔵 Benedict's Solution Test (Reducing Sugar)

Add Benedict's solution (blue) to the sample and heat gently in a water bath.

✅ Positive: colour changes from blue → green → yellow → orange → brick-red precipitate.
❌ Negative: stays blue.

🟣 Biuret Test (Protein)

Add biuret solution (or sodium hydroxide solution followed by a few drops of copper sulfate solution) to the sample at room temperature — no heating needed.

✅ Positive: colour changes from blue to purple/violet (lilac).
❌ Negative: stays blue.

⚪ Ethanol Emulsion Test (Fats and Oils)

Shake the food sample with ethanol, then pour the mixture into a test tube of water.

✅ Positive: a cloudy white emulsion forms.
❌ Negative: the mixture stays clear.

🟡 DCPIP Test (Vitamin C)

Add the food sample drop by drop to blue DCPIP solution, counting the drops needed.

✅ Positive: DCPIP loses its blue colour (is decolourised). Fewer drops needed = MORE vitamin C present.
❌ Negative: DCPIP stays blue even after many drops.

Phase 4 · 4.1 Structure of DNA

🧬 The Structure of DNA

DNA carries the genetic instructions in every cell. Its structure follows a small number of simple, exact rules.

Double Helix Bases Bonds Between Bases Base Pairing Rule
Double Helix — A DNA molecule is made of two strands, coiled together to form a double helix (a twisted-ladder shape).
Bases — Each of the two strands contains a sequence of chemicals called bases, attached along its length.
Bonds Between Bases — Bonds between pairs of bases (one base from each strand) hold the two strands of the DNA molecule together, like the rungs of a ladder.
Base Pairing Rule — The bases always pair up in the same way: A pairs with T, and C pairs with G (full chemical names are not required at IGCSE).

📝 IGCSE Past Paper Questions

These questions follow the style and content of CIE IGCSE Biology past papers (0610). Write your answers, then click Check All Answers to reveal the mark-scheme level answers.

CIE IGCSE Biology — Chemical Elements · 2 marks
State the chemical elements that make up carbohydrates, and state ONE element found in proteins that is NOT found in carbohydrates. [2]
Tip: Carbohydrates need three elements listed; proteins have one extra.
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • Carbohydrates: carbon, hydrogen and oxygen [1]
  • Proteins additionally contain nitrogen [1]
CIE IGCSE Biology — Building Blocks · 2 marks
Starch, glycogen and cellulose are all built from the same smaller molecule. Name this molecule, and state which of the three is found in plant cell walls. [2]
Tip: Think about which one gives plant cells structural support.
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • Glucose [1]
  • Cellulose [1]
CIE IGCSE Biology — Food Tests · 2 marks
A student adds iodine solution to a food sample and it turns blue-black. They then add Benedict's solution to a second sample of the same food and heat it; it stays blue. What do these two results tell you about the food? [2]
Tip: One test is positive, the other is negative — what does each result mean?
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • The food contains starch (positive iodine test — blue-black) [1]
  • The food does not contain reducing sugar (Benedict's test remained blue — negative) [1]
CIE IGCSE Biology — Biuret Test · 2 marks
Describe how you would carry out the biuret test on a food sample, and state the colour change that indicates a positive result. [2]
Tip: This test does NOT require heating.
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • Add biuret solution (or sodium hydroxide solution followed by a few drops of copper sulfate solution) to the sample at room temperature [1]
  • A positive result is shown by a colour change from blue to purple/violet (lilac) [1]
CIE IGCSE Biology — DCPIP Test · 2 marks
Explain why needing fewer drops of a food sample to decolourise DCPIP solution indicates a HIGHER concentration of vitamin C in that sample. [2]
Tip: Think about what is actually reacting with the DCPIP.
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • DCPIP is decolourised by vitamin C (vitamin C reacts with and removes the blue colour) [1]
  • If fewer drops are needed to decolourise the DCPIP, each drop must contain more vitamin C, i.e. the sample has a higher concentration [1]
CIE IGCSE Biology — DNA Base Pairing · 2 marks
One strand of a DNA molecule has the base sequence: A T T C G A. State the sequence of bases on the strand that would pair with it. [2]
Tip: Apply the rule A–T and C–G to each base in order.
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • Correct application of the base-pairing rule (A pairs with T; C pairs with G) [1]
  • Correct sequence: T A A G C T [1]
CIE IGCSE Biology — DNA Structure · 2 marks
Describe how the two strands of a DNA molecule are held together, and describe the overall shape of the molecule. [2]
Tip: One mark for the bonds, one mark for the overall 3D shape.
Mark Scheme Answer (2 marks):
  • Bonds between pairs of bases hold the two strands together [1]
  • The two strands are coiled together to form a double helix [1]
